How to use these These are reference pages, not the module notes. They are here for when you are stuck on one idea and want it set out from the beginning, in one place, with the derivation written out and the figures redrawn. Nothing in the module requires you to read them, and nothing in them is assessed that is not also in the notes — so treat them as the thing you reach for when something has not landed, rather than as another reading list.

Chapter 15 (University Physics) · Oscillations

  • 15a
    Harmonic Oscillators and Hooke's Law

    A spring pulls back harder the further you stretch it, and that single fact — a restoring force proportional to displacement — is enough to force sinusoidal motion.

  • 15b
    Describing Harmonic Motion

    Once you know an oscillation is sinusoidal, three numbers pin it down completely: how far it swings, how long one swing takes, and where in the cycle it happened to be when you started the clock.

  • 15c
    Harmonic Motion and Uniform Circular Motion

    Simple harmonic motion is not merely like circular motion — it is circular motion, viewed from the side.

  • 15d
    Energy in Oscillating Systems

    An oscillator holds a fixed amount of energy and keeps moving it between two accounts.

  • 15e
    The Simple Pendulum, with worked examples

    A pendulum is not obviously a harmonic oscillator: the restoring force goes as sin θ, not as the displacement itself.

  • 15f
    Two-Spring Systems: Parallel and Series

    Put two springs on one mass and you do not have to start over: the pair behaves as a single spring with one effective stiffness.

  • 15g
    Finding Information in a Graph

    A graph of measured data will not usually hand you the quantity you want.

Chapter 16 (University Physics) · Waves

  • 16a
    Waves: Vocabulary, Speed and the Wave Equation

    A wave carries energy from one place to another without carrying the material with it.

  • 16b
    Wave Speed on a String

    How fast a wave runs along a string is fixed by the string, not by whoever plucks it.

  • 16c
    Energy and Power in Waves

    A wave moves energy, and this page works out how much. Take one small piece of the string, find its kinetic and potential energy as an oscillator, and add up every piece along one wavelength.

  • 16d
    Interference of Waves

    Two waves can occupy the same place at the same time, and when they do you simply add their displacements.

  • 16e
    Standing Waves and Harmonics

    Fix a string at both ends and it will not vibrate at just any frequency.

Chapter 17 (University Physics) · Sound

  • 17a
    Sound Waves and the Speed of Sound

    Sound is a longitudinal wave in a material. The air does not travel from the speaker to your ear; it squeezes and spreads in place, and the squeeze travels.

  • 17b
    Sound Intensity and the Inverse Square Law

    A source puts out a fixed number of watts. As the sound travels outward, those watts are spread over a larger and larger sphere, so the amount arriving on any given patch falls away — not because the source is weakening, but because the sound is getting thinner.

  • 17c
    Sound Intensity Level: the Decibel

    The quietest sound a person can hear and the loudest one they can survive differ by a factor of about ten thousand billion.

  • 17d
    Beats

    Play two notes that are nearly but not quite the same and you do not hear two notes.

  • 17e
    The Doppler Effect, with worked examples

    A siren sounds higher as it comes towards you and lower as it goes away, and the horn itself never changes.
