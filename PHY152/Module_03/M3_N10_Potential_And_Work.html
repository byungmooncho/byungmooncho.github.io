Which is the eV unit doing exactly what the synopsis said it would: (elementary charges) × (volts), and no arithmetic with 10−19 anywhere in sight.

  1. Moving 4.0 coulombs of charge through a circuit requires 48 joules of electric energy.  What is the potential difference across this circuit?

WE = qV48 J = (4 C)VV = 12 V

  1. If instead 8.0 coulombs was brought into the field, how would the electric potential change, if at all?

No.

That is his whole answer, and it is the point of the second set.  The potential is what the field offers per coulomb; it is already there before you bring anything into it.  Double the charge and you double the work, not the potential — exactly as doubling the mass on the right-hand ladder in Figure 1 doubles the joules at every rung and changes gh not at all.
