The oil drop

Base your answers to questions 13 through 15 on the information and diagram below and on your knowledge of physics.

The diagram shows a negatively charged oil drop that is suspended motionless between two oppositely charged, parallel, horizontal metal plates.  The electric field strength between the charged plates is 4.0 × 104 newtons per coulomb.  The 1.96 × 10−15‑kilogram oil drop is being acted upon by a gravitational force, Fg, and an electrical force, Fe.

Figure 15.  The drop hangs still, so the two forces on it are equal and opposite.  The two labels are his, written on the diagram: Fe = qE upward and Fg = mg downward.
  1. Calculate the magnitude of the gravitational force, Fg, acting on the oil drop.  [Show all work, including the equation and substitution with units.]

Fg = mg = (1.96 × 10−15 kg)(9.8 m/s²)= 1.92 × 10−14 N

  1. Determine the magnitude of the upward electrical force, Fe, acting on the oil drop suspended motionless between the charged metal plates.

Fg = Fe = 1.92 × 10−14 N

It is suspended motionless, so there is nothing to work out — the upward force has to match the downward one exactly.

  1. Calculate the net electric charge on the oil drop in coulombs.  [Show all work, including the equation and substitution with units.]

Fe = qE1.92 × 10−14 N = q (4 × 104 N/C)q = (1.92 × 10−14 N) / (4 × 104 N/C) = 4.8 × 10−19 C

And beside it he wrote the same thing in one line:

qE = mg
