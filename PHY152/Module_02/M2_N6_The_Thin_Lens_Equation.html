The Thin Lens Equation

SynopsisOne equation locates the image, a second says how big it is and which way up, and a short table of signs makes both of them work for every lens in the module — converging or diverging, image real or virtual.  The equations are two lines.  The signs are the note.
Before you start — predict

The same 3 cm object is put in front of the same 10 cm converging lens three times: at 50 cm, at 5 cm, and at 20 cm.  One of those three produces an image that is upright.  Which one, and why that one?

Performance expectations
  1. Apply 1/do + 1/di = 1/f to locate an image.
  2. Apply m = −di/do to get its size and orientation.
  3. Read the sign of each quantity correctly, and say what each sign means physically.
  4. Recognise from a negative di that the image is virtual, and say which side of the lens it is on.

2.  Thin lens equation

Figure 1.  Converging lens, object outside the focal point.  The three rays are not placed by eye: the image sits where 1/do + 1/di = 1/f puts it, and all three cross there because the arithmetic forces them to.
1/do + 1/di = 1/f

and the magnification — image height over object height — is

m = −di / do

The signs

do — object distancereal objectvirtual object
di — image distancereal imagevirtual image

Four rows, and every question in this note is answered by reading them in the right order: signs in, arithmetic, signs out.

Worked example

Find the location, orientation and magnification of the image of a 3 cm high object placed at (a) 50 cm, (b) 5 cm and (c) 20 cm in front of a convex lens of 10 cm focal length.

  1. Work all three before you open the solution.  The arithmetic is four keystrokes each; the sign of the answer is the part worth checking.
(a) do = 50 cm, f = +10 cm.  1/50 + 1/di = 1/10, so 1/di = 0.100 − 0.020 = 0.080 and di = +12.5 cm. m = −12.5/50 = −0.25 — real, inverted, one quarter of the object’s size (0.75 cm high). (b) do = 5 cm.  1/5 + 1/di = 1/10, so 1/di = 0.100 − 0.200 = −0.100 and di = −10 cm. m = −(−10)/5 = +2 — virtual, upright, twice the size (6 cm high), on the same side of the lens as the object.  The object is inside the focal length: that is the magnifying glass. (c) do = 20 cm.  1/20 + 1/di = 1/10, so 1/di = 0.100 − 0.050 = 0.050 and di = +20 cm. m = −20/20 = −1 — real, inverted, same size.  Object at 2f puts the image at 2f on the other side, life size.  Worth remembering as a landmark.

What about a concave lens?

Nothing new.  The same two equations, with f entered as a negative number.

Figure 2.  A concave lens, worked the same way.  Nothing about the equation changes — only the signs you feed it.  f < 0 comes out as di < 0, and a negative image distance means the image is on the same side as the object: virtual, upright, and smaller.
The punchline 1/do + 1/di = 1/f    m = −di/do

Back to the prediction: the upright image is (b), at 5 cm — the one case where the object sits inside the focal length.  That is not a special equation; it is the same equation returning a negative di.
