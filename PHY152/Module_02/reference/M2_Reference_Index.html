Module 2 Reference Pages

Web versions of the lecture slides by Dr. H. Trevor Johnson-Steigelman (Physics This Week), adapted for PHY 152.

How to use these These are reference pages, not the module notes. They are here for when you are stuck on one idea and want it set out from the beginning, in one place, with the derivation written out and the figures redrawn. Nothing in the module requires you to read them, and nothing in them is assessed that is not also in the notes — so treat them as the thing you reach for when something has not landed, rather than as another reading list.
Where they came from Each page is a web version of one or two of Trevor’s lecture decks, transcribed faithfully, with every figure redrawn as a scalable diagram and the chapter numbering matched to University Physics. His attribution and licence travel with each page.

Chapter 1 (University Physics) · The Nature of Light

  • M2R1
    The Nature of Light and the Law of Reflection

    Nobody can quite say what light is — that argument runs into quantum mechanics and does not come out.

  • M2R2
    Snell's Law and Total Internal Reflection

    A ray crossing from one medium into another bends, and the amount it bends by is fixed entirely by the two indices of refraction.

Chapter 2 (University Physics) · Geometric Optics and Image Formation

  • M2R3
    The Lens Maker's and Thin Lens Equations

    Two equations, and they answer different questions. The lens maker’s equation is about the piece of glass: grind these two curves into this material and you get that focal length.

  • M2R4
    Ray Tracing and Two-Lens Systems

    Three rays behave so simply that you can draw them without calculating anything, and where they cross is where the image is.

Chapter 3 (University Physics) · Interference

  • M2R5
    Thin-Film Interference

    A soap bubble is colourless soapy water, and it comes out rainbow.

Chapter 4 (University Physics) · Diffraction

  • M2R6
    Intensity in Single-Slit Diffraction

    Knowing where the dark fringes fall is one question; knowing how bright the pattern is at some arbitrary angle is another, and it needs a different equation.

  • M2R7
    Unravelling DNA: a public lecture

    This one is not a lesson — it is a talk, and it is kept whole because it is worth keeping whole.
