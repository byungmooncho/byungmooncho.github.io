The Lens Maker’s Equation

SynopsisA lens is two curved refracting surfaces back to back, and a ray crossing each one bends by Snell’s law — the law of Note 3, nothing new.  Do that twice and out falls a single number, the focal length, written in terms of the two radii and the glass.  This note builds that equation and then throws most of it away.
Before you start — predict

Two lenses are ground from the same glass to the same shape, but one is made twice as thick through the middle.  Do they have the same focal length?  Commit to an answer.   Then read the last line of this note.

Performance expectations
  1. Identify R on a lens surface as the radius of the circle that surface is a piece of.
  2. State the lens maker’s equation and say what each of f, R1, R2, n1, n2 and d stands for.
  3. Reduce it to the thin-lens-in-air form, and say exactly which two assumptions you used.
  4. Compute a focal length from two radii and a refractive index.

1.  Lens maker’s equation

A ray of light travelling through a curved surface will bend according to Snell’s law.  That is the entire physical input.  Everything below is geometry applied to it twice — once going in, once coming out.

R = radius of curvature, which describes the edge of the refracting surface.

Figure 1.  Every curved surface in this note is a piece of a circle, and R is that circle’s radius — drawn here from each real centre of curvature out to its own real arc.  Both arcs above have the same radius; they get opposite signs in the equation only because they bulge in opposite directions.

Carrying Snell’s law across both surfaces and collecting the terms gives the focal length of the lens:

1/f = (n2/n1 − 1) [ 1/R1 − 1/R2 + (n2 − 1)d / (n2R1R2) ]
  • f — focal length of the lens
  • R1 — radius of curvature of lens side 1
  • R2 — radius of curvature of lens side 2
  • n1 — refractive index of the medium in which the object sits
  • n2 — refractive index of the lens
  • d — thickness of the lens

The form you will actually use

For a thin lens in air, two things are true at once: d << R, so the last term inside the bracket is negligible; and n1 = 1, so n2/n1 is just the index of the glass, n.  Both assumptions together collapse the equation to

1/f = (n − 1) [ 1/R1 − 1/R2 ]
The punchline 1/f = (n − 1) [ 1/R1 − 1/R2 ]

Back to the prediction: the thickness d dropped out.  Two lenses of the same glass and the same curvature have the same focal length however thick they are through the middle — as long as both are thin enough for d << R to hold.  Make one thick enough and the term comes back.

Looking ahead

The lens maker’s equation is about grinding a lens — glass and curvature in, focal length out.  From Note 6 onward the focal length is simply given to you, and the question becomes where the image lands.

Check yourself

2.  Which two assumptions take the full equation to 1/f = (n − 1)[1/R1 − 1/R2]?
