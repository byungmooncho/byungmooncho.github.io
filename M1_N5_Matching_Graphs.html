Constant Speed Motion: Matching Graphs to Motion Scenario

Synopsis Motion graphs quantitatively encapsulate an object's motion — you can read off its position and speed as a function of time. In this note you'll go both directions: read a motion graph and describe the motion in words (how far, forward or backward, how fast), and sketch a motion graph from a written description of an object's motion.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1Give a quantitative description of an object's motion, segment by segment, from a distance–time or speed–time graph.
  • PE2Sketch a distance–time graph from a written, segment-by-segment description of an object's motion.
  • PE3Sketch the corresponding speed–time graph (and recognize the acceleration–time graph is flat at zero throughout, since each segment is constant speed).
  • PE4Determine net displacement ("where am I?") and final speed from a multi-segment motion description.
graph ↔ words, both directions
💡You already know this

You already translate step-by-step descriptions into a mental picture of where something ends up:

🚕 A trip log "Drove 2 miles, waited at a light, drove 3 more" — you track distance segment by segment.
🎮 A video game character's path Right for 2 steps, pause, left for 1 step — you can picture exactly where they end up.
📖 Following a recipe One step at a time, and at any moment you know exactly where you are in the process.

In this note, we work through

  1. How do I describe an object's motion, segment by segment, from a graph?
  2. How do I sketch a distance–time graph from a written description?
  3. How do I get the speed–time (and acceleration–time) graph from the same description?
  4. How do I figure out "where am I?" and "how fast am I going?" at the end?

Reading the shape of a segment

Distance–time segment looks like…Means the object is…
Sloping upwardMoving forward
Sloping downwardMoving backward
Flat (horizontal)At rest (stopped)
Steeper slopeMoving faster

Looking ahead: every segment here is still constant speed — straight lines only. Starting in Note 6, individual segments themselves can curve, because the object accelerates within a segment rather than switching abruptly between constant speeds.
