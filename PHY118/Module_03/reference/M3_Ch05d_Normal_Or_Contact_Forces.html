Normal or Contact Forces

Web version of the lecture slides by Dr. H. Trevor Johnson-Steigelman (Physics This Week), adapted for PHY 118.

The idea A table holds a book up. Nothing about the table decided to do that — the force it supplies is whatever is needed, in whatever direction the surface faces. Two habits follow. First, the normal force points perpendicular to the surface, not upward, so a wall pushes sideways and a ramp pushes at an angle. Second, its size is not a property of the object; it is an unknown you solve for, which is why it is only sometimes equal to the object’s weight.

“Normal” means perpendicular

It is a maths word, not a physics word In mathematics normal is the generic term for perpendicular. The normal force is the force of contact between two objects, and it is perpendicular to the surface of contact. Calling it a contact force means exactly the same thing.

An object on a frictionless incline

Rotate the coordinate system so that the x-axis is parallel to the incline and the y-axis is perpendicular to it. Gravity then splits into a component along the surface and a component into it:

Two directions, two equations
y-direction (perpendicular to the surface) x-direction (parallel to the surface)
m ay = FN + Fg⊥ m ax = Fg∥
m ay = FN − Fg⊥ m ax = Fg sin θ
0 = FN − Fg⊥   (it does not leave the surface) —
FN = Fg cos θ ax = g sin θ

Rotating the axes is what buys this. In the tilted frame the object does not accelerate at all in y, so that equation collapses to a statement about FN alone.

Check yourself

Assuming that there is no other force than gravity acting, the magnitude of FN is always…

  1. Equal to Fg
  2. Equal to or less than Fg
  3. Greater than Fg
  4. Has no relation to Fg
Answer: 2. FN = Fg cos θ, and cos θ ≤ 1 for any real tilt. Equality holds only on level ground, where θ = 0. Tip the surface and the normal force falls off; stand it vertical and it vanishes.

Read the caveat carefully. “No other force than gravity” is doing real work in that sentence. Lean on the object, or put it in an accelerating elevator, and FN can exceed Fg without limit.

The normal force does not have to point up

Hold a book against a wall. The wall is the surface, so the contact force points horizontally, away from the wall. Gravity still pulls straight down, and friction — parallel to the surface, as always — is what holds the book from sliding.

Be careful: the weight and the normal force are not always the same

The sentence this whole page exists for On level ground, with nothing accelerating, FN happens to equal Fg. That coincidence is so common that students start treating it as the definition of the normal force. It is not. Tilt the surface and FN drops by cos θ; turn the surface vertical and it is set by your push instead; accelerate the floor and it changes again. It is also what a bathroom scale is actually reading — see page 05e.

Review

  • The normal force is the force of contact between two objects.
  • It is perpendicular to the surface — “normal” is the mathematical word for perpendicular.
  • It does not have to point straight up. In fact it often does not.
  • It is not the object’s weight. It is an unknown you solve for from Newton’s second law in the perpendicular direction.
