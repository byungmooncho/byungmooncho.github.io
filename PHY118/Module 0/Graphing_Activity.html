Graphing Activity — Hand Plot vs. Excel Plot

Goal You will plot the same position-vs-time data twice — once by hand on grid paper, and once in Excel — find the slope of the best-fit line both ways, and compare the two results in a short write-up. The slope of a position-vs-time graph is the speed, so both methods should give you nearly the same number.

1Hand plot on grid paper

You will receive a sheet of grid paper in class (a printable copy is linked below). Work in pencil first, then darken your final line.

  1. Set up your axes. Look at the data table and decide the range each axis must cover: time runs from 0 to 1.00 s, position from 0 to about 2.0 m. Choose a scale that spreads the data across most of the page — a tiny graph squeezed into one corner is hard to read and hard to grade. Time goes on the horizontal axis; position on the vertical axis.
  2. Label both axes with units. Write Time (s) along the horizontal axis and Position (m) along the vertical axis, and mark evenly spaced tick values (0.1, 0.2, 0.3, … on the time axis, for example).
  3. Give the graph a title. Write a descriptive title at the top, such as Position (m) vs. Time (s).
  4. Plot the data as a scatter plot. Place a small, neat point (a dot or ×) at each of the ten data pairs. Do not connect the dots point-to-point.
  5. Draw the best-fit line with a ruler. The best-fit line is a single straight line drawn through the cloud of points so that the points scatter evenly above and below it. It does not have to pass through any particular data point — it represents the overall trend.
  6. Find the slope — and show your work on the plot. Pick two points on your line (not data points!) that are far apart. Circle them, label them (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂), and draw the rise-and-run triangle on the graph, just like the figure below. Then compute
    slope = rise / run = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)
    and write the result with its unit next to the triangle. Since rise is in meters and run is in seconds, your slope is in m/s — it is the speed.
Why two far-apart points? Reading two points that are close together magnifies small reading errors. Points near the two ends of your line give the most reliable slope.
Hand-plot checklist — every item is graded

2Excel plot of the same data

Now make Excel do the same job. Download the Excel data file below — it already contains the data table — and follow the instructions on the sheet:

  1. Select the data. Highlight the two columns, including the Time (s) and Position (m) headers.
  2. Insert a scatter chart. Go to Insert → Charts → Scatter and choose the plain scatter (markers only, no connecting lines).
  3. Add title and axis labels. Use Chart Design → Add Chart Element to add axis titles with units and a chart title, matching your hand plot.
  4. Add a linear trendline. Right-click a data point and choose Add Trendline…. Select Linear, and check both Display Equation on chart and Display R-squared value on chart.
  5. Read off the slope. The equation appears in the form y = mx + b. The number multiplying x is the slope — attach the unit m/s when you report it.
Check yourself Your hand-plot slope and Excel's slope should agree to within a few percent. If they are wildly different, one of the two plots has a problem — find it before you write up your results.

3Write up and submit

Download the Word template below. It has four sections — fill them in in order, replacing the italic instructions in each:

  1. Hand Plot. Take a photo of your grid-paper plot with your cell phone — hold the phone flat over the page in good light so the title, the axis labels with units, your best-fit line, and the rise-and-run slope work are all clearly legible. Send the photo to yourself (email or OneDrive), then insert it in the Hand Plot section via Insert → Pictures. Click the inserted image, open the small icon at its top right, and choose In Line with Text.
  2. Excel Table and Graph. In Excel, select the data table, right-click, and choose Copy. In the template's Excel Table and Graph section, right-click and choose Paste as Picture (the option with the mountain icon). Do the same for the chart, placing the graph beside the table, not under it, and set each image to In Line with Text. Do not link the table or graph — linking causes problems.
  3. Observe and Comment. Write a few sentences: state the Excel best-fit equation, report the slope (the speed) from Excel and the slope from your hand plot — both with units — and describe how the two compare.
  4. Neaten, save, and submit. Keep the document to two pages. Delete all italic instruction text, spell-check, and put your name and the date in the heading. Save the file as a PDF named Graphing Activity - Your Name.pdf (using your own first and last name) and submit it to the assignment folder in Brightspace. We submit PDFs because embedded graphs in .docx files often break in the LMS.
