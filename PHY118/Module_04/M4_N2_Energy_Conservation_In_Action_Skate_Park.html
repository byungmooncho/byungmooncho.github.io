Energy Conservation In a Skate Park

SynopsisIn this note, you will utilize the PhET simulation to observe the skater’s kinetic (KE) and potential energy (PE) as the skater skates on a ramp.  Are the changes in KE and PE related?  If so, how?

First, simulate the ramp with no friction.  Then, turn on the friction and see how it affects KE, PE, and the total energy.

  3. Click the plus sign next to the Energy button on the left to display the KE, PE, Thermal, and Total energy levels bar chart.
  4. Check the “Pie Chart,” “Speed,” “Path,” and “Stick to Track” boxes on the options panel on the right.
  5. Place the skater at the top of the half-pipe, and watch what happens to the skater, and to the PE, KE, Thermal, and total energy.

No friction: the bar charts

Pause the motion at the top and at the bottom.  The chart shows four energies: energy of motion (kinetic), energy due to the skater’s height above ground (gravitational potential), total mechanical energy (kinetic + gravitational potential), and thermal energy (heat generated by friction).  With no friction the three snapshots look like this:

Read them across.  At the top, KE is zero and PE is at its maximum; at the bottom it is the other way round.  The thermal bar stays at zero the whole time — there is no friction to make any.  And the total bar never moves: it is the one that stays constant ALL THE TIME.  Total mechanical energy = KE + PE = constant.

The speed dial says the same thing.  At the top the speed is zero; at the bottom it is at its maximum.  Zero v, max PE, max h at the top; max v, zero PE, zero h at the bottom.  Speed and height trade places, which is what KE and PE trading places looks like.

With friction

Slide the friction from “None” to a mid-range value and pause at the same three places.

Now the thermal bar grows on every pass, and the mechanical bars shrink by exactly that amount.  The skater slows down and eventually stops at the bottom (Q5).  She stops because her mechanical energy, KE + PE, has all converted to thermal energy (Q6) — and thermal energy is useless for getting back up the ramp.  On the way down, PE turns into KE with a little leaking to thermal, so the KE gained is less than the PE lost; on the way up, KE turns into PE with the same leak, so the PE gained is less than the KE lost (Q7).  Either way, mechanical energy goes down.  Define the total energy as PE + KE + TE, and that total bar still does not move.

In your guided notes: sketch the three charts in the item 10 cells — mark the “lost” part of each mechanical bar — and answer Q5, Q6 and Q7.

Summarizing

Q8.  With no friction, total mechanical energy KE + PE is constant: it stays finite, and it stays forever.  It is useful energy — it can still be work.

Q9.  Friction generates thermal energy — energy that is no longer capable of work.  Degraded energy; dissipation; “wasted” energy.

Q10.  Friction converts mechanical energy into thermal energy until, eventually, no more work is possible.

So, in one line, for the ride without friction:

KE + PE = constant — without friction
ΔKE + ΔPE = 0 — the same statement about changes
(KEf − KEi) + (PEf − PEi) = 0 — your gain is my loss, and vice versa

A zero-sum game — that is what a conservation law is.  Note 3 solves this equation for speeds and heights.

Bonus: move the reference height

Set the friction back to “None”, check “Reference Height”, and drag the dashed line up until it sits above the bottom of the pipe (the instrument above has the same control).  Below the line the PE bar goes negative, and the total drops by the same amount — and still stays flat.  The skater’s motion does not change at all.  A negative potential energy only means “below the level you chose to call zero”; only changes in PE are physical, and those did not change.

Where this sits. Note 1 named the three energies; this note watches them trade.  Note 3 writes KE + PE = constant as an equation and solves it; Note 5 takes the thermal bar seriously.
Back to your prediction

With friction on, three bars change and one does not: the total — KE + PE + thermal.  If you said “KE + PE”, that is the one friction eats; if you said “none”, you were thinking energy is destroyed, and it is only degraded.
