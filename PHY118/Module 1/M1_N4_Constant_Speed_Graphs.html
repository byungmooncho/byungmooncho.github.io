Constant Speed Motion: Graphs of Constant Speed Motion

Synopsis We describe motion two ways: distance vs. time (position as a function of time) and speed vs. time. In constant-speed motion the distance–time graph is a straight line whose slope is the speed — the faster you go, the steeper the line — and because the slope never changes, there is no acceleration. The speed–time graph is a flat, horizontal line, and the acceleration–time graph sits on zero the whole time.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1Recognize that constant-speed motion has zero acceleration — the velocity does not change over time.
  • PE2Interpret a distance–time graph as a straight line whose constant (non-zero) slope is the speed, and read that slope as rise over run.
  • PE3Recognize the speed–time graph for constant speed as a flat, horizontal line (zero slope) sitting at a non-zero speed.
  • PE4Recognize the acceleration–time graph for constant speed as a flat line on zero for all time.
  • PE5Sketch all three graphs (distance, speed, acceleration vs. time) for a described constant-speed scenario.
  • PE6Connect the slope idea to algebra — in y = mx, the slope m = Δd/Δt is exactly the speed.

💡You already know this

The slope of a distance–time graph isn't new physics — it's the algebra you already know, dressed up with units:

📈 y = mx from algebra The slope m is rise over run. Here that's Δd/Δt — meters per second — which is speed.
🚗 Cruise control Steady on the highway: the speedometer holds one number, so speed–time is a flat line.
🏃 A steady jog Same pace the whole way — no speeding up or slowing down means zero acceleration.

The three graphs at a glance

For constant-speed motion, each graph has a signature shape. If you can picture these three, you can read or sketch any constant-speed scenario.

GraphShape for constant speedWhat its slope tells you
Distance vs. timeStraight line, tilted up (or down if moving back)slope = speed (constant, non-zero)
Speed vs. timeFlat, horizontal line at the speed valueslope = 0 → speed isn't changing
Acceleration vs. timeFlat line sitting on zeroa = 0 for all time

Looking ahead: here every graph is a straight line because the speed never changes. Starting in Note 6 we let the speed change — and the distance–time graph curves, the speed–time graph tilts, and the acceleration–time graph lifts off zero. Master the straight-line case first; it's the anchor everything else is measured against.
