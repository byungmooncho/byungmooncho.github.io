Accelerating Motion: Graphs of Accelerating Motion (e.g., Freefall)

Synopsis A free-falling object doesn't fall at a constant speed — a ticker tape shows the dot spacing growing, meaning it speeds up as it falls. That's accelerated motion. The distance–time graph is no longer a straight line — it curves — because the slope (speed) keeps changing. The velocity–time graph is still a straight line, but now with a non-zero slope: that slope is the acceleration. For free fall, that acceleration is a constant g = 9.8 m/s², and with downward defined as negative, a = −g.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1Recognize that the distance–time graph is curved in accelerated motion — its slope is not constant, because the speed itself is changing.
  • PE2Recognize the velocity–time graph as a straight line with a constant slope, equal to the (constant) acceleration.
  • PE3Determine whether an object speeds up or slows down by comparing the signs of its velocity and acceleration: same sign speeds up, opposite signs slow down.
  • PE4Apply this to free fall: explain why a = −g (downward-negative convention) means a downward-moving object necessarily speeds up.

💡You already know this

You've felt acceleration — both speeding up and slowing down — your whole life:

🎢 A roller coaster launch Pressed back into your seat — that push is your body responding to a large, forward acceleration.
🚗 Braking hard Thrown forward — the acceleration points opposite your motion, so you slow down.
🍎 A dropped apple Newton's classic example — it speeds up the whole way down under gravity's constant pull.

  4. Why does a negative acceleration (like −g) make a downward-moving object speed up rather than slow down?

Preamble: work through these first

Before sketching, reason through the following in your guided notes:

  1. Speed is distance per time. If speed increases, the distance covered in each equal time interval also increases — so the distance–time graph must curve upward.
  2. Because speed increases at a constant rate, the speed–time graph is a straight line with a constant slope — and that slope equals the acceleration.
  3. For a falling object with downward defined as negative: since velocity and acceleration point the same way (both downward, both negative), the object speeds up — it does not slow down.
