Scalar and Vector Quantities

Synopsis A scalar quantity only needs a number to describe it — direction doesn't make sense for it. A vector quantity needs both a number (how large) and a direction (which way), so you need an arrow to represent it. In this note you'll sort a long list of physics quantities into these two categories.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1 Distinguish scalar quantities (magnitude only) from vector quantities (magnitude and direction).
  • PE2 Classify common physics quantities — distance, displacement, speed, velocity, force, and more — as scalar or vector.
  • PE3 Explain why direction matters for some quantities (displacement, velocity, force) but not others (distance, speed, mass).

💡You already know this

You already separate "how much" from "which way" without thinking about it:

📏 "How far did you walk?" vs. "Where did you end up?" Total distance covered is one thing; your net position relative to where you started is another.
🚗 A speedometer vs. a compass The speedometer gives a number; the compass gives a direction. Velocity needs both.
💪 "Push harder" vs. "push that way" Force has a size, but it also always points somewhere.
