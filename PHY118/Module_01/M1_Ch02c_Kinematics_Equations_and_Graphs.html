Kinematics Equations and Graphs

Web version of the lecture slides by Dr. H. Trevor Johnson-Steigelman (Physics This Week), adapted for PHY 118.

The idea The kinematics equations are not arbitrary formulas — each one is the shape of a graph. The position equation is a parabola, the velocity equation is a straight line, and constant acceleration is a flat line. Better still, the position equation can be read off a velocity–time graph as the area “under” the line: a rectangle plus a triangle.

Acceleration vs. time graph

In Physics I, we usually treat the acceleration as constant. In reality, it usually isn't — a car doesn't jump instantly to full acceleration, and air resistance changes as things speed up. But constant acceleration is a good approximation for a lot of motion, and it is the only case the three kinematics equations describe.

Look at the area “under” the graph

Pick two moments t1 and t2 on the velocity graph. The area between the line and the time axis splits into a rectangle (height v1) and a triangle on top (height v2 − v1). Drag the sliders and watch the pieces.

The total area always equals the change in position read from the position graph — that is the point.
“Area” units — why area on this graph is a distance
base × height  →  s × ms = m

Seconds cancel, meters remain. The “area” on a velocity–time graph is not a geometric area at all — it is a displacement.

Rectangle

A▭ = base × height
A▭ = (t2 − t1) × (v1 − 0)
A▭ = Δt × v1
A▭ = v1Δt

Triangle

A△ = ½ base × height
A△ = ½(t2 − t1) × (v2 − v1)
A△ = ½Δt Δv
A△ = ½Δt Δv × ΔtΔt (multiply by 1)
A△ = ½ ΔvΔt (Δt)² and Δv/Δt is the acceleration
A△ = ½a(Δt)²

Finding the total area “under” the graph

Total area = the position equation
AT = A▭ + A△
AT = v1Δt + ½a(Δt)²
Δx = v1Δt + ½a(Δt)² (the area is the displacement)
x − x0 = v1Δt + ½a(Δt)²
x = x0 + v1Δt + ½a(Δt)²

Here v1 is the velocity at the start of the interval — the same thing the position equation calls v0. The kinematics equation for position is nothing more than “rectangle plus triangle.”

Graphs where an object travels up and then down

Throw something upward: it rises, stops, and falls back past where it started. The position graph is an upside-down parabola; the velocity graph is a straight line that crosses through zero.

The area “under the graph” is really the area between the function and the time axis. Where the velocity is positive the area counts as positive displacement; where the velocity is negative the area counts as negative displacement — the object is moving back toward (and past) where it started.

Concept question On the velocity graph above, the positive area from 0 to 10 s and the negative area from 10 to 20 s are the same size. What is the object's displacement over the first 20 s?

Displacement vs. distance, again Adding the areas with their signs gives the displacement (zero at 20 s). Adding their sizes gives the distance traveled (up 25 m and back down 25 m = 50 m). Same graph, two different questions — exactly the distinction from slides 02a.

Review

The position equation, x − x0 = v1Δt + ½a(Δt)², is made up of the…

  • Initial starting position (distance from x = 0) — x0.
  • Distance travelled if it had moved at a constant velocity — the rectangle, v1Δt.
  • Extra distance due to acceleration — the triangle, ½a(Δt)².

The area “under” the velocity vs. time graph is the change in position.

  • Areas above the time axis are positive.
  • Areas below the time axis are negative.
