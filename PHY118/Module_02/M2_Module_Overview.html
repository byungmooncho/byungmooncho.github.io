Motion in Two Dimensions

What this module is about Module 1 followed things moving along a line. Almost nothing actually does. A thrown ball, a car turning, a plane in a crosswind — all of them move in two directions at once, and the equations you already know cannot describe them as they stand. This module builds the one tool that fixes that: the vector. Once you can split a vector into perpendicular pieces, a two-dimensional problem stops being new physics and becomes two Module 1 problems side by side.
You already know this The whole mathematical toolkit for this module is the right triangle — the Pythagorean theorem and SOH-CAH-TOA. Nothing new is asked of your trigonometry. What is new is what you point it at.

How the module runs

NoteWhat it does
N1How Do Vectors Add? Why vectors do not add like ordinary numbers, and the head-to-tail rule that says how they do.
N2Resolving A Vector. Head-to-tail run backwards — one slanted vector split into perpendicular x and y pieces, then added component by component.
N2aTrig Definitions. The right-triangle mechanics that make resolving exact instead of drawn: SOH-CAH-TOA, signs by quadrant, and recombining.
N3Freefall vs. Horizontal Launch. The payoff — horizontal and vertical motion are independent, so a launched ball and a dropped ball land together.

Work them in that order. N2a is the toolkit for N2, so treat the two as a pair rather than separate topics.

By the end of this module you will be able to…
  • 1Add two or more vectors head-to-tail, and find the resultant's magnitude and direction by measurement.
  • 2Resolve a vector into perpendicular components using Fx = F cos θ and Fy = F sin θ.
  • 3Assign each component the correct sign from the quadrant the vector actually points into — a reference angle alone will not tell you.
  • 4Add vectors analytically by summing x-components and y-components separately, then recombine into a resultant.
  • 5Explain why the horizontal and vertical motions of a projectile are independent, and argue it from evidence.
  • 6Solve horizontally launched projectile problems — including finding fall time from drop height alone.

The one habit worth building now Draw the triangle before you reach for the calculator. Almost every error in this module is a sign error or a swapped sine and cosine, and almost every one of those is caught by a sketch that takes ten seconds.
