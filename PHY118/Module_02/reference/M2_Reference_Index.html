Module 2 Reference Pages

Web versions of the lecture slides by Dr. H. Trevor Johnson-Steigelman (Physics This Week), adapted for PHY 118.

How to use these These are reference pages, not the module notes. Each one is a self-contained walk-through of one slide deck — worked examples, figures you can look at as long as you like, and a short self-check at the end. Read the note for a topic first; come here when you want the idea explained a second way, or when you are working a problem and need to see the method laid out step by step. Nothing here is graded.

Chapter 3 — Vectors

  1. 03a Vector Basics What makes a quantity a vector, magnitude and direction, and adding arrows head-to-tail. Parallel, antiparallel, perpendicular.
  2. 03c Vector Components Resolving one slanted arrow into perpendicular x and y pieces with SOH-CAH-TOA, and going back the other way with Pythagoras and the inverse tangent.
  3. 03d Vector Addition Using Components The exact route: resolve every vector, add the x’s and the y’s separately, then rebuild the resultant. Two one-dimensional problems instead of one two-dimensional one.
  4. 03e Vectors in Other Quadrants What changes when a vector leaves the first quadrant — negative components, and getting the direction angle right rather than whatever the calculator hands back.

Chapter 4 — Projectile Motion

  1. 04a Horizontally Fired Projectiles The independence of horizontal and vertical motion, worked on the simplest case: launched level, falling from the moment it leaves your hand.
  2. 04b General Projectile Problems Launching at an angle: resolve the initial velocity, run x and y separately, and deal with the quadratic that the vertical equation hands you.
  3. 04c Special Equations for Projectiles The apex-height and range formulas derived from the component equations — where they come from, and the level-ground assumption that makes them legal.

One caution on the shortcuts The range and maximum-height formulas on page 04c only hold when the projectile lands at the same height it was launched from. Off a cliff, off a table, into a basket — go back to the component equations on 04b. A formula used outside its conditions is worse than no formula.
