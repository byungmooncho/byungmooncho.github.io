Trig Definitions

Synopsis In the last note, we resolved a vector into a right triangle: the vector itself is the hypotenuse, and its x- and y-components are the two legs. That gave us the picture — F = Fx + Fy. What it didn't give us were numbers. SOH–CAH–TOA is what turns that picture into arithmetic: sine, cosine, and tangent are just fixed ratios between a right triangle's opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse sides. Once we know which side is which, Fx = F cosθ and Fy = F sinθ fall right out — and running the same idea backward, we can recombine two vectors' components into a single resultant's magnitude and direction. The one catch: the calculator only knows the angle you give it, not which way the vector actually points. You have to supply the sign yourself.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1State SOH–CAH–TOA and identify a right triangle's opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse sides relative to a given angle θ.
  • PE2Compute a vector's components using Fx = F cosθ (adjacent) and Fy = F sinθ (opposite).
  • PE3Assign the correct sign to each component based on which quadrant the vector actually points into — a reference angle alone doesn't tell you the sign.
  • PE4Recombine components into a resultant — magnitude |C| = √(Cx² + Cy²) and direction θ = tan⁻¹(…) — from two vectors' Fx/Fy values.
💡You already know this

SOH–CAH–TOA isn't new — you've used right-triangle ratios without the vector dressing:

🪜 A leaning ladder The ladder is the hypotenuse. How far it reaches up the wall (opposite) versus how far its base sits out (adjacent) depends only on the angle it leans at.
📺 TV screen size A "55-inch" TV is measured along the diagonal — the hypotenuse. The actual width and height are the adjacent and opposite legs of that same triangle.
🛹 A skate ramp Steeper angle, more "opposite" (rise) for the same ramp length (hypotenuse), and less "adjacent" (run). Same triangle, same three ratios.

Worked example: recombining two vectors

Vector A (10 m/s) is joined head-to-tail with vector B (5 m/s), the same way we joined vectors in Note 1. This time, instead of measuring with a ruler and protractor, we'll find the resultant C by the numbers — resolve each vector into components, add the components, then recombine.

Σ Worked example · A = 10 m/s ∠60°, B = 5 m/s ∠30°
Step 1 · Resolve A (Quadrant IV — right and down) A's reference angle is 60° from the horizontal. Pointing into Quadrant IV, its x-component is positive and its y-component is negative: Ax = +A cos60° = +(10)(0.500) = +5.00 m/s Ay = −A sin60° = −(10)(0.866) = −8.66 m/s
Step 2 · Resolve B (Quadrant III — left and down) B's reference angle is 30° from the horizontal — but this time B points into Quadrant III, so both components are negative, even though cos30° and sin30° are themselves positive numbers. The sign comes from the diagram, not the calculator: Bx = −B cos30° = −(5)(0.866) = −4.33 m/s By = −B sin30° = −(5)(0.500) = −2.50 m/s
Step 3 · Add components (this is the whole reason to resolve!) Components along the same axis add like ordinary numbers — that's the payoff of breaking each vector into x- and y-pieces first: Cx = Ax + Bx = (+5.00) + (−4.33) = +0.67 m/s Cy = Ay + By = (−8.66) + (−2.50) = −11.16 m/s Cx comes out positive and Cy comes out negative — matching the diagram, where the resultant leans slightly right of straight down.
Step 4 · Recombine into magnitude and direction Now run SOH–CAH–TOA in reverse. Cx and Cy are the legs of a new right triangle; |C| is its hypotenuse. Because the resultant sits almost straight down, it's most natural here to measure θ from the vertical axis instead of the horizontal one — pick whichever axis makes the triangle easiest to see: |C| = √(Cx² + Cy²) = √(0.67² + 11.16²) = 11.18 m/s θ = tan⁻¹(|Cx| / |Cy|) = tan⁻¹(0.67 / 11.16) ≈ 3.4° Reading the signs of Cx (+) and Cy (−) tells us the full picture: C points mostly straight down, tilted just 3.4° toward +x.
Resultant C ≈ 11.2 m/s, 3.4° from vertical (toward +x)
