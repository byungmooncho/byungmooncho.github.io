Guesstimation and Measurement of Length, Mass, and Time

Synopsis Length (m), mass (kg), and time (s) are the three fundamental quantities that combine to build every other physical quantity — velocity is length/time, acceleration is velocity/time, force is mass·acceleration, and energy is force·distance. In this hands-on lab, you'll build an intuitive "feel" for how long, massive, and fast everyday things really are: first guess in meters, kilograms, and seconds (MKS units), then check yourself with a ruler, balance, and stopwatch. Physicists lean on this kind of intuition constantly to sanity-check calculations.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1 Know the rough ranges of lengths and masses of typical objects and the time durations of events around us.
  • PE2 Guesstimate then measure a quantity, and compute the %error between your guess and the measured value.
  • PE3 Determine a walking speed from a measured distance and time, then use it to predict the time for a new distance.

Putting it together: the elevator walk

Using your average stride length, estimate the distance from your classroom to the elevator. Then measure your walking speed by timing yourself across the known length of the classroom. Use that speed to predict how long the walk to the elevator will take — then time yourself walking there and compute the %error between your prediction and the real result.

Looking ahead: the %error formula here is exactly the one from Note 2a, and the walking-speed calculation is your first real use of distance = velocity × time — the same relationship that anchors Note 3's introduction to kinematics.

Key relationships for this note

  • 🎯 %error = |guess − measured| / measured × 100 how far off your guesstimate was, as a percentage
  • 🚶 speed = distance / time measure your own walking speed across a known distance
  • ⏱️ time = distance / speed use your measured speed to predict a new travel time
