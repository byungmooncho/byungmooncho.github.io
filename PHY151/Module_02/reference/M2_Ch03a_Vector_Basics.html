Vector Basics

Web version of the lecture slides by Dr. H. Trevor Johnson-Steigelman (Physics This Week), adapted for PHY 151.

The idea Some quantities need only a number and a unit — 3 kg, 20 °C, 45 s. Others are useless until you also say which way: a 5 m/s wind out of the west is a different thing from a 5 m/s wind out of the north. Those are vectors, and they come with their own arithmetic. Two vectors of 1.0 m do not have to add to 2.0 m — the answer depends entirely on the angle between them.

You already know this A right triangle relates two perpendicular sides to a hypotenuse. That is the entire mathematical toolkit for this chapter: the Pythagorean theorem and SOH-CAH-TOA. Nothing new is being asked of your trigonometry — only that you apply it to arrows.

Math you should know down pat

Warning “Opposite” and “adjacent” are not properties of the sides — they are properties of the angle you chose. Switch your attention from θ to φ and the two sides trade names: x becomes the opposite side and y the adjacent one. This is exactly why you should never memorize “cosine goes with x.”

Which quantities are vectors?

A vector has magnitude and direction. A scalar has magnitude only. These come up again and again in this course:

“As the crow flies”

Drive from Victor to Canandaigua and your odometer reads about 25 km. But the trip displaced you only 21.6 km, along a straight line pointing southeast. Three different routes give three different distances — 24.6 km, 28.0 km, 32.4 km — and all three give the same displacement.

Writing a vector

d⃗→An arrow over the symbol says “this is a vector.” Some books use bold d instead.
d≡The same letter with no arrow means the magnitude only — a plain positive number with units.
Handwriting Draw the arrow pointing right, always. A “backwards” arrow over the symbol is not a different vector, it is just hard to read — and on an exam it costs you the benefit of the doubt.

Same numbers, different vector

A table top is 1.5 m by 2.5 m and stands 0.8 m tall. Stand the same three measurements up differently — 1.5 m by 0.8 m, standing 2.5 m tall — and you have a completely different piece of furniture. The numbers did not change; the directions they point did.

The same bookkeeping runs a chess board. A rook that moves one square right and two squares up has made the displacement x̂ + 2ŷ: one step in the x direction, two steps in the y direction.

Those little “hatted” symbols are unit vectors, the subject of the next page in this set (03b).

Vectors as ordered pairs of numbers

The “length” of a vector

Drop a right triangle under the arrow and the Pythagorean theorem gives its size:

Magnitude from components
d⃗ = (4, 2) m   →   |d⃗| = √(4 m)² + (2 m)² = √20 m² ≈ 4.5 m
v⃗ = (3, 0) m/s   →   |v⃗| = 3 m/s

Note the units on the second line. The “length” of a velocity vector is not measured in metres — which is why physics says magnitude, a generic word for size, instead of “length.” It is also why you cannot compare a displacement arrow and a velocity arrow by how long they look on the same diagram.

Notation you will meet

Magnitude≡The size of the vector. Most books write it |d⃗| — some use double bars instead — and many just drop the arrow and write plain d.
Direction≡An angle, usually measured from the x-axis and usually given a Greek letter: θ, φ, α, β.

Magnitude and direction together are the polar description of a vector; the ordered pair (x, y) is the Cartesian one. Both describe the same arrow, and Chapter 3 is largely about moving between them.

Parallel, antiparallel, perpendicular

The trap “Add the magnitudes” is a special case, not a rule. Two 1.0 m displacements add to 2.0 m only if they are parallel; to 0 m if antiparallel; and to √2 ≈ 1.4 m if perpendicular.

Review

  • A vector carries magnitude and direction; a scalar carries magnitude only. Displacement, velocity, acceleration, and force are vectors — distance, speed, mass, and time are not.
  • The same arrow can be written two ways: as an ordered pair (x, y) (Cartesian) or as a magnitude and an angle (polar). The Pythagorean theorem and SOH-CAH-TOA are what move you between them.
  • A vector does not care where it is drawn. Same length, same direction means same vector.
  • Vectors add head-to-tail (or by parallelogram) to give the resultant. Magnitudes add arithmetically only when the vectors are parallel, and subtract only when antiparallel.
