How Do Vectors Add?

Synopsis Displacement and velocity are vector quantities — they need both a magnitude and a direction, and each is the vector "counterpart" of a scalar you already know (distance and speed). Scalars add like ordinary numbers. A vector is like an arrow: it has a length and points somewhere, and arrows do add, subtract, and multiply (never divide — vector division doesn't exist), just not the way numbers do. The rule is head-to-tail: join the head of one vector to the tail of the next, and the sum — the resultant — runs from the tail of the first vector to the head of the last. A negative sign simply flips a vector's head and tail, so subtraction is addition with one arrow reversed.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1Distinguish scalar from vector quantities and give examples of each — and recognize that an arrow's length is the magnitude and its head is the direction.
  • PE2Add vectors head-to-tail — join the head of one vector to the tail of the next, keeping each vector's orientation.
  • PE3Draw the resultant vector from the tail of the first vector to the head of the last, and determine it diagrammatically with a ruler and protractor.
  • PE4Subtract vectors by flipping the negative vector's head and tail (it points the opposite way), then adding head-to-tail as usual.
  • PE5Rejoin vectors that are drawn tail-to-tail or separated in space — move them without rotating them until they connect head-to-tail, then add.
  • PE6Relate the joint angle to the resultant's length — vectors pointing the same way give the longest resultant; doubled back gives the shortest.

💡You already know this

You've been adding vectors your whole life — you just never drew the arrows:

🚶 Walking directions "Three blocks east, then four blocks north." Two legs of a trip, joined end to end — your total displacement is the straight arrow from start to finish.
🪢 Tug-of-war Two teams pull in opposite directions. Equal pulls cancel to nothing; direction matters, not just effort.
✈️ Crosswind A plane aims north while the wind pushes east — it actually travels along the diagonal. Two motions combine into one.

The joint angle at a glance

When two vectors of lengths a and b connect head-to-tail, the angle at the joint sets the resultant's length. (Remember the note's convention: a straight-ahead connection makes a joint angle of 180°.)

How they joinJoint angleResultant length
Straight ahead — same direction180°a + b — the longest possible
At a corner — perpendicular90°between the extremes (diagonal of the corner)
Doubled back — opposite direction0°a − b — the shortest possible
Doubled back, equal lengths0°zero — you end where you started

Three rules to remember

🔗 Head-to-tail Join the head of one vector to the tail of the next — rejoining them if they're drawn tail-to-tail or apart, without rotating them.
🎯 R: first tail → last head The resultant starts at the tail of the first vector and ends at the head of the last.
↔️ −B flips B A negative sign swaps head and tail — the vector points the opposite way. Subtraction is just addition of the flipped vector.
