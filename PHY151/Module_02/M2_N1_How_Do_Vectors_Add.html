How Do Vectors Add?

Synopsis Displacement and velocity are vector "counterparts" of distance and speed — they carry a direction as well as a size. Most quantities you'll meet are one or the other, vector or scalar, with no matching partner. Scalars add the way ordinary numbers do. Vectors don't: a vector is an arrow, and arrows add by joining the head of one to the tail of the next — the head-to-tail rule. A negative sign flips a vector's head and tail, pointing it the opposite way, which is how vector subtraction works. In this note we learn to add and subtract vectors diagrammatically; multiplication comes later.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1Distinguish scalar addition from vector addition — scalars add like ordinary numbers; vectors add head-to-tail.
  • PE2Apply the head-to-tail rule to join two or more vectors, keeping each vector's own magnitude and orientation.
  • PE3Recognize that a negative sign flips a vector's head and tail — it reverses direction without changing length. That's how subtraction works.
  • PE4Draw the resultant vector — from the tail of the first vector to the head of the last — for two or more vectors joined head-to-tail.
  • PE5Determine a resultant's magnitude and direction by measurement (ruler and protractor) for vectors joined at any angle.
  • PE6Recognize when vectors must be rejoined — two vectors drawn joined at the tail (or head), or separated in space, must be moved head-to-tail before you can add them.
💡You already know this

Head-to-tail addition isn't new — you've been doing it without the vocabulary:

🗺️ Turn-by-turn directions "3 blocks east, then 2 blocks north." Each leg starts where the last one ended — that's head-to-tail — and where you end up is the resultant.
🎮 Game-controller movement Press right, then up. Your character's net move is the head-to-tail sum of the two presses, even though you made them one at a time.
🔄 Walking it back Walk 5 blocks east, then 5 blocks west — the second leg is the negative of the first. You end up exactly where you started.
