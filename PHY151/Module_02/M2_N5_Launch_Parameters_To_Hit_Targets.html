Determining Launch Parameters to Hit Targets

Synopsis So far we've launched first and asked where the projectile lands. Real ballistics runs the problem backwards: the target is given, and you must determine the launch parameters — velocity and angle — that hit it. In this activity your launcher fires from the bench table at targets placed 1 m, 2 m, and 3 m away. First you'll measure the launch velocity (and check your method against the photogate), then calculate the launch angles from the launch velocity, launch height, and target distances — and then fire for the record. Hitting your targets earns 10% bonus credit on the Module 2 Exam.
Performance Expectations By the end of this activity, you will be able to…
  • PE1Describe and apply a method to determine the launch velocity of a real launcher using only what Module 2 has taught you.
  • PE2Compare your measured velocity with the photogate's and argue which measurement is more reliable, and why.
  • PE3Set up the inverse problem — identify the knowns (launch velocity, launch height, target distance) and the unknown (launch angle).
  • PE4Calculate launch angles to hit targets at 1 m, 2 m, and 3 m, showing the calculation for each.
  • PE5Recognize that a reachable target usually admits two solutions — a low, fast arc and a high, lobbed arc.
  • PE6Verify by firing — compare the predicted and actual landing points and account for the difference.

💡You already know this

Solving for the launch instead of the landing is something you already do by feel:

🗑️ The office trash shot You don't compute where the paper ball lands — you pick the arc that lands it in the bin. That's an inverse problem.
⛳ Club selection A golfer knows the ball's speed off each club; the question is which loft covers this distance.
🎪 Cornhole & free throws Underhand lob or flat toss — two very different arcs can drop into the same hole.

In this activity, we work through

  1. Describe a method to determine the launcher's launch velocity — then apply it.
  2. The launcher's photogate independently reports the launch velocity. Compare the two values: which measurement is more reliable, and why?
  3. How does the launch height (the bench table) enter the calculation?
  4. Calculate the launch angles needed to hit targets at 1 m, 2 m, and 3 m.
  5. Why can two different angles hit the same target?
  6. Fire for the record — how close were your predictions, and what explains the difference?

Three equations that aim the launcher

✂️ Vx = V cos θ · Vy = V sin θ Resolve the measured launch velocity at your trial angle — Note 2a, as always.
⏱️ −h = Vyt − ½gt² The landing condition: the ball ends a height h below the bench-top launch point. Solve for the flight time t.
🎯 dx = Vx·t Where it lands. Choose θ so this equals your target distance — 1 m, 2 m, or 3 m.
