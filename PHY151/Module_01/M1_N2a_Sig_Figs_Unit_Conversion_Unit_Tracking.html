Significant Figures in Calculations, Unit Conversion, and Unit Tracking

Synopsis Every measurement has a precision limit set by your instrument. This note covers three connected skills: reading measurement uncertainty and expressing it as % error; the rules for keeping the right number of sig figs when you add, subtract, multiply, or divide measured numbers; and unit conversion and unit tracking — confirming an equation is dimensionally correct by following its units through.
Performance Expectations
  • PE1Determine measurement uncertainty and express it as % error; distinguish precision from accuracy.
  • PE2Apply sig-fig rules correctly when adding/subtracting vs. multiplying/dividing.
  • PE3Convert between metric units and confirm an equation's units track correctly.

Reading a ruler: precision and uncertainty

A ruler with 1 mm markings limits you to about ±0.5 mm precision. Below, the line's end falls between the 59 and 60 mm marks — you estimate one digit beyond the smallest scale: 59.8 ± 0.5 mm.

Uncertainty as % error: %error = (δX / X) × 100. Here, δX = 0.5 mm, X = 59.8 mm → (0.5/59.8)×100 = 0.8%.

Precision vs. accuracy

Accuracy error compares a measured value to a theoretical/expected one: %error = (|Measured − Expected| / Expected) × 100. Example: theory predicts g = 9.8 m/s²; you measure 10.2 m/s² → %error = (0.4/9.8)×100 ≈ 4.1%.

Rule of thumb: ×, ÷ → result keeps the fewest sig figs of the inputs. +, − → result keeps the least precise decimal place. Example: 59.893 m × 34.32 m = 2055.52267 m² → rounds to 2056 m² (4 s.f., since 34.32 has only 4).

Unit conversion

General form: (# in starting unit) × (conversion factor) = # in desired unit. Example: convert 1.76 miles to m, using 1 mile = 1609.34 m → 1.76 × 1609.34 = 2830 m (3 s.f.).
