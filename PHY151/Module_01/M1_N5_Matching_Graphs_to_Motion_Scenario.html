Constant Speed Motion: Matching Graphs to Motion Scenario

Synopsis Motion graphs quantitatively encapsulate the motion of an object — we can learn the position and speed of the object as a function of time. In this note you will 1) describe the motion in words from a motion graph (distance vs. time), and 2) sketch a motion graph from a written description of an object's motion.
Performance Expectations
  • PE1Give a quantitative description of an object's motion from a motion graph.
  • PE2Draw the motion graph from a description of an object's motion.
In calculus notation

The instantaneous velocity at any moment is the derivative of position, v(t) = dx/dt — the slope of the tangent line to the distance-vs-time graph at that instant. For the piecewise motions below, v(t) is a piecewise-constant function of time, and position is recovered by integrating it:

x(t) = x₀ + ∫₀t v(t′) dt′

Geometrically, that integral is just the running signed area under the velocity-vs-time graph — exactly what the "Where am I?" questions below ask you to compute.
