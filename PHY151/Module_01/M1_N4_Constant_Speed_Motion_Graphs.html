Constant Speed Motion: Graphs of Constant Speed Motion

Synopsis In constant-speed motion, the distance-vs-time graph is a straight line — its slope is the speed. The faster the speed, the steeper the line, but the slope never changes: no speeding up, no slowing down, no acceleration. The speed-vs-time graph is simply a flat line — constant and unchanging.
Performance Expectations
  • PE1Recognize that constant-speed motion gives a distance-vs-time graph with constant (nonzero) slope.
  • PE2Recognize that the speed-vs-time graph is a flat line at zero slope — zero acceleration.
In calculus notation

Velocity is the instantaneous rate of change of position: v = dx/dt. For constant-speed motion, that derivative is itself constant, so the second derivative — the acceleration — is zero:

dx/dt = constant   —   a = d²x/dt² = 0
