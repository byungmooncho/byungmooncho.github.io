Scales of Nature

Synopsis We explore objects in the universe from very small to extremely large. Because the range of numbers spanned is unimaginably large, we describe the sizes in terms of orders of magnitude — the number of zeros in the number.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1Describe quantities in order-of-magnitude scales (10 to a power) rather than exact numbers.
  • PE2Compare sizes and masses across many orders of magnitude, from a quark to the observable universe.
  • PE3Use ratios of powers of ten to answer "how many times bigger/smaller" and "how many of these fit inside that" questions.
💡You already know this

You already reach for "order of magnitude" thinking without calling it that:

🏙️City vs. countryA city has ~1 million people; a country can have ~100–1000x more — you don't need the exact count to compare scale.
💵"Six figures"People describe salaries or prices by number of digits — that's an order-of-magnitude shorthand already.
🔬Ant vs. elephantYou instantly know these differ by orders of magnitude in size and mass, without weighing either one.
