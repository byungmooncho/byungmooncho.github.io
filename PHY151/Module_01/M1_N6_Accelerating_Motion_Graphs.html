Accelerating Motion: Graphs of Accelerating Motion (e.g., Freefall)

Synopsis A freefalling object does not fall at constant speed — it speeds up as it falls; this is accelerated motion. The distance-vs-time graph is curved (not a straight line), because the slope of that graph is speed, and speed itself is changing. The velocity increases linearly with time, so the velocity-vs-time graph is a straight line — its constant slope is the acceleration. For freefall, that constant acceleration is g = −9.8 m/s².
Performance Expectations
  • PE1Recognize that the distance-vs-time graph is curved in accelerated motion, and its slope is not constant.
  • PE2Recognize that velocity (the slope of distance-vs-time) varies linearly with time, so the velocity-vs-time graph is a straight line with constant slope equal to the acceleration.
