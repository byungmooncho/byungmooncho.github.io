Energy Conservation In a Skate Park

Synopsis In this note, you will utilize the PhET simulation to observe the skater’s kinetic (KE) and potential energy (PE) as the skater skates on a ramp. Are the changes in KE and PE related? If so, how?

First, simulate the ramp with no friction. Then, turn on the friction and see how it affects KE, PE, and the total energy.
Performance ExpectationsBy the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1Describe energy conversion on a half-pipe: where the skater’s energy is kinetic, where it is potential, and how one becomes the other.
  • PE2State what is conserved — with no friction, KE + PE is the same number at every point of the ride.
  • PE3Account for friction — mechanical energy leaves, thermal energy appears, and the total of all three still does not change.

For PHY 151 - with calculus

The conservation statement is an equation you can differentiate

With no friction the bar chart says E = ½mv² + mgh = constant along the whole ride. A constant has zero time derivative, so d/dt(½mv² + mgh) = 0. Carry out the derivative with the chain rule and you get mv·(dv/dt) + mg·(dh/dt) = 0. Along the track, dh/dt = v·(dh/ds) where s is distance along the track, so after dividing by mv the statement becomes dv/dt = −g·(dh/ds): the acceleration along the track is minus g times the local slope. That is Newton’s second law for the skater, and it came out of “total energy is constant” without drawing a single force. It is also the F = −dU/dx of Note 1 in disguise.

the derivative of a constant
