Dissipative Forces and Thermal Energy

Web version of the lecture slides by Dr. H. Trevor Johnson-Steigelman (Physics This Week), adapted for PHY 151.

The idea Friction does not destroy energy — it moves it somewhere you cannot get it back from. Slide a block to a stop and its ordered, whole-body kinetic energy has become disordered kinetic energy of individual atoms. That is thermal energy, and the reason it is a one-way trip is that you would have to re-aim every atom to reverse it.
You already know this Friction as Ff = μFN from Module 3, work from Ch07a, the work–energy theorem from Ch07b, and the conservative / non-conservative split from Ch08b. This page picks up exactly where Ch08b stops.

Macroscopic and microscopic energy

The same words — kinetic energy — apply at two scales, and keeping them apart is what makes the rest of the page work.

Two scales, one idea
Macro(scopic) energyMicro(scopic) energy
Kinetic energy due to motion of the object as a whole.
Kmacro = ½Mv2		 Kinetic energy of the atoms.
Katom = ½m vatom2, with m = M/N (N = number of atoms)
Kmicro ≈ N Katom
Eth = Kmicro + Umicro

Thermal energy is the atoms' kinetic energy plus the potential energy of the bonds between them. Trevor labels this “internal” energy and ties it to temperature — and flags, in parentheses, that thermal energy is not heat. Heat is energy in transit because of a temperature difference; thermal energy is what a body already has.

Friction turns one into the other

Pull a box across a floor at a constant speed. The net force is zero, so none of your work is going into kinetic energy. It is all going somewhere else.

Calculating the thermal energy
ΣF⃗ = m a⃗  →  Fp − Ff = 0  →  Fp = Ff
Wnc = F⃗f · Δd⃗ = Ff Δd cos 180°
Wnc = −Ff Δd
ΔEth = +Ff Δd

Trevor asks on the slide: what does the negative mean? It means the mechanical account is being debited. The thermal account is credited by the same amount, which is why ΔEth carries the opposite sign. Nothing is lost; the two numbers are one transaction seen from either side.

You can feel this one Use sandpaper, or run a drill, and the objects heat up. That is not a side effect of the friction — it is the friction, seen at the scale of the atoms. The energy you are putting in with your arm is arriving in the surface as faster jiggling.

Slowing down because of friction

Now let the pull be weaker than the friction, so the box actually slows. The full energy statement has three terms, and it is worth writing out once in full before collapsing it.

The whole balance, then the useful form
ΔE = Wp + ΔEth + ΔK
ΔE = F⃗p · d⃗ + F⃗f · d⃗ + ΔK
ΔE = Fpd cos 0° + Ffd cos 180° + ΔK
0 = Fpd − Ffd + ½m vf2 − ½m vi2
ΔK = Ffd − Fpd

Read the last line as a competition. The pull adds kinetic energy, friction removes it, and whichever is larger over the distance d decides whether the box speeds up or slows down. Set Fp = 0 and you have the braking-car result from Ch07b; set them equal and you are back at the constant-speed case above.

Why friction gets no potential energy You could, in principle, collect every jiggling atom's motion and turn it back into bulk motion of the block. Nothing in the laws forbids it. What makes it hopeless is that you would have to aim them all the same way at once, and there are vastly more disordered arrangements than ordered ones. That is the whole content of “friction is non-conservative” — and it is the thread you will pick up again in thermodynamics.

Review

Thermal energy— Eth = Kmicro + Umicro. Related to temperature. Not the same as heat.
Dissipative forces— friction and drag. They convert macroscopic kinetic energy into microscopic.
The transfer— ΔEth = +Ff Δd, the same size as the mechanical energy lost.
One-way— not because energy vanishes, but because the motion has become disordered.
