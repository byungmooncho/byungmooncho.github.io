Module 4 Reference Pages

How to use these These are reference pages, not the module notes. They are here for when you are stuck on one idea and want it set out from the beginning, in one place, with the derivation written out and the figures redrawn. Nothing in the module requires you to read them, and nothing in them is assessed that is not also in the notes — so treat them as the thing you reach for when something has not landed, rather than as another reading list.
Where they came from Each page is a web version of one or two of Trevor’s lecture decks, transcribed faithfully, with every figure redrawn as a scalable diagram and the chapter numbering matched to University Physics. His attribution and licence travel with each page.

Chapter 7 (University Physics)

  • 07a
    Work and the Dot Product

    Everyday “work” means effort. Physics work is narrower and it is the narrowness that makes it useful: only the part of a force that lies along the motion counts.

  • 07b
    Kinetic Energy and the Work–Energy Theorem

    Kinetic energy is not a new postulate. Take the constant-acceleration equation you have had since Module 1, multiply through by the mass, and the combination ½mv2 falls out on its own — with the work on the other side.

  • 07c
    Work Done by Variable Forces

    W = Fd cos θ assumes the force holds still while the object moves.

  • 07d
    Power and Efficiency

    Work does not care how long you took. Carry a box up three flights in a minute or in an hour and the work is the same number.

Chapter 8 (University Physics)

  • 08a
    Gravitational Potential Energy

    Lift something and you do work against gravity. Let it go and you get that work back as motion.

  • 08b
    Conservative and Non-Conservative Forces

    Carry a box from A to B by two different routes and ask how much work gravity did.

  • 08c
    Dissipative Forces and Thermal Energy

    Friction does not destroy energy — it moves it somewhere you cannot get it back from.

Chapter 9 (University Physics)

  • 09a
    Momentum and Impulse

    Newton's second law has a second reading. Instead of “force equals mass times acceleration”, write it as “force equals the rate of change of mv” — and a new conserved quantity appears, along with a tool for every collision problem.

  • 09b
    Energy in Collisions

    Momentum is conserved in every collision. Kinetic energy is not — and how much of it survives is what distinguishes one kind of collision from another.
