Momentum Conservation – One of Nature’s Most Strictly Enforced Laws

SynopsisAlong with energy and matter conservation, which you are perhaps familiar with from chemistry, the conservation of momentum law is one of Nature’s laws that one has never seen or will ever see being violated.  As a matter of fact, you can build a theory on the premise of this conservation law and make predictions based on the fact that you can count on this law to be observed.

Just as one can never create or destroy matter or energy (the total is always the same), nor can one create or destroy momentum – the total momentum before and after an event must be the same, i.e., conserved.

In this Note, I will present one of the most important equations in physics, which describes the conservation of momentum.  You will observe a few examples of the conservation law in action and see how the equation summarizes the conservation phenomena.

When I say an event in the context of momentum conservation, I mean a collision – two bodies in motion coming into contact.  When there is contact, that is a collision event.  One of two things will happen: they will either collide and separate, or they will stick together and move as a unit.  When they bounce, it’s called an elastic collision.  When they stick, it’s called an inelastic collision.

We will begin with demonstrations of elastic collisions using the smart carts and Newton’s Cradle.

Newton’s Cradle

Lift one ball and let it fall against the row: one ball leaves the far end at the same speed.  Lift two, two leave.  The balls in the middle never seem to move at all; the momentum passes straight through them.

  1. Stare at the hypnotic bouncing back and forth of a row of hanging steel balls in Newton’s Cradle.  How is the conservation of momentum law manifested?  Draw a diagram showing the velocity vectors before and after.

BeforeAfter

One ball in with velocity v; the other four at rest.  ptotal = mv + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 = mv.

The four it hit are at rest again and the last ball leaves at v: ptotal′ = 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + mv = mv.  The same momentum, handed down the row ball by ball.  Two in, two out — 2mv both times.  (One in and two out at half speed would also conserve momentum; it is the kinetic energy that rules it out — the 151 fence.)

The equation

Everything above is one sentence of algebra: add up the momenta before, add them up after, set the sums equal.  The equation does not care whether the carts bounced or stuck — only the total appears in it.

  1. Write the equations for momentum before and after.
  2. Write the general momentum conservation equation when two bodies collide.
BeforeAfter
BeforeAfter

ptotalbefore = pA + pB = mAvA + mBvB

ptotalafter = pA′ + pB′ = mAvA′ + mBvB′

Elastic (bounce):  mAvA + mBvB = mAvA′ + mBvB′
Inelastic (stick):  mAvA + mBvB = (mA + mB)v′ — one velocity after, because they move as a unit

The equation with numbers

Two smart carts on the level track, east positive.  Cart A is 2.0 kg and cart B is 1.0 kg.  Set each case up on the instrument above after you have worked it.

  1. Cart A moves east at 3.0 m/s and hits cart B, at rest.  They stick.  Write the total momentum before, and find the speed they move off with together.
  2. Same start, but this time they bounce and cart A is measured afterwards at 1.0 m/s east.  How fast is cart B moving, and which way?
  3. Cart A at 3.0 m/s east meets cart B at 3.0 m/s west, and they stick.  What happens?
i) before: (2.0)(3.0) + (1.0)(0) = 6.0 kg·m/s.  after: (2.0 + 1.0)v′ = 6.0 → v′ = 2.0 m/s east — two-thirds of A’s speed, not half: B is the lighter cart
ii) 6.0 = (2.0)(1.0) + (1.0)vB′ → vB′ = 4.0 m/s east — B left faster than A ever moved; the total is still 6.0
iii) before: (2.0)(3.0) + (1.0)(−3.0) = +3.0 kg·m/s.  after: 3.0v′ = 3.0 → v′ = 1.0 m/s east.  They do not stop: the heavier cart’s momentum wins, and the pair creeps east.
For PHY 151 — with Newton’s laws

Why the total cannot change.  During the contact the carts push on each other with equal and opposite forces (third law), FAB = −FBA, and they are in contact for the same time Δt.  Newton’s second law in the form F = Δp/Δt gives ΔpA = FABΔt and ΔpB = FBAΔt, so ΔpA = −ΔpB and the sum pA + pB changes by nothing at all.  Nothing in the argument mentions kinetic energy, which is why the total momentum is the same whether the carts bounce or stick — the kinetic energy is not.

  1. Write the argument as three lines of algebra ending in pA + pB = pA′ + pB′.
  2. For the sticking collision in section 4, calculate the kinetic energy before and after.  Where did the difference go?
i) FAB = −FBA (third law);  ΔpA = FABΔt = −FBAΔt = −ΔpB;  so (pA′ − pA) + (pB′ − pB) = 0, i.e. pA + pB = pA′ + pB′.
ii) KE before = ½(2.0)(3.0)² = 9.0 J;  after = ½(3.0)(2.0)² = 6.0 J.  3.0 J became thermal energy (and sound) in the coupling — momentum conserved, mechanical energy not.  That is what “inelastic” costs.
