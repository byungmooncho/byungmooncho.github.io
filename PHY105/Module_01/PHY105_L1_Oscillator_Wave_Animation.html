Watch what each knob does. Turn the frequency up: the mass bobs faster and the repeats crowd together — the wavelength shrinks, because the medium refuses to change its speed. Now grab the mass and pull it farther before letting go: the amplitude grows — bigger swings, stronger disturbances — but the spacing between repeats does not move. Pushing up instead of pulling down works too; the wave simply starts on the opposite half of its cycle. The source sets f. The medium sets v. Nobody chooses λ — it is whatever v/f says it must be.

Too slow to hear. The air really would carry this pattern exactly as shown — but a mass bobbing once or twice per second is far below the roughly 20 repeats per second your ear needs before it calls a vibration “sound.” A guitar string does the same thing this mass does, just hundreds of times per second. Same physics, different frequency — and audibility itself is a frequency question. More on that in Unit 3.
