Hearing Loss Article Reaction Paper

SynopsisModule 3 has been about putting numbers on sound — intensity, the decibel, how levels add, and how loudness is not the same as level. This assignment asks what those numbers cost you. You read one article by an audiologist who works with musicians, and you write two pages reacting to it.

  3. The addenda, linked as PDFs at the foot of the article. Addendum A covers particular instruments — find yours, or the one you stand nearest. Addendum B covers specific hearing protection. 3 · What a reaction paper is

Read this before you start writingA reaction paper is not a summary. Summarise only as much as you need to set up the point you are reacting to — a sentence or two — and spend the rest of the two pages on what you think and what you will do. A paper that only retells the article will not score well.

4 · What to discuss

These three are the spine of the paper. Give each one real space.

Things you learned from the article. Pick the two or three things that actually changed your picture, and say why. A fact you already knew does not count for much here.

Things you currently do to protect your ears. Be honest and specific. “I turn it down sometimes” is not an answer; “I use the volume limit on my phone and I stand away from the PA” is.

Things you will do in the future to protect your ears. Name something you will actually do, and say what in the article convinced you. If the article did not convince you of anything, say that and defend it.

5 · Where this meets the physics

You already measured the thing this article is about. In N2 you put sounds on the decibel scale, and in N3 you found that the safe limit is 85 dB for eight hours, and that every +3 dB halves the time. Look at where a rock concert, a chainsaw and a school dance sit on the chart below, then work out how long the exposure limit actually is at each one. That number is the argument the article is making.

The article makes one point that should land differently now that you have done N4: damage shows up near the frequency of whatever caused it, and the band your ear is most sensitive to is also the band that goes first. That is why hearing loss takes speech before it takes anything else.
