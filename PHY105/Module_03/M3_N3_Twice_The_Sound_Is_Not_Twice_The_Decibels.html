Twice the Sound Is Not Twice the Decibels

SynopsisLast class you measured sound levels with your phone, and in N2 you met the decibel. This note asks what happens when sounds combine and when you move away from them. Because the decibel scale counts powers of ten, a second singer adds only about 3 dB, and doubling your distance from a speaker drops the level by about 6 dB. We finish with the question every musician should ask: how loud is too loud, and for how long?
Performance ExpectationsPE1 Place a sound on the decibel ladder: convert between intensity (W/m2) and level (dB) using powers of ten. PE2 Predict how the level changes when the number of identical sources changes (×2, ×10). PE3 Predict how the level changes when your distance from a source doubles or grows ×10. PE4 Estimate a safe listening time from a sound level using the 85 dB / 3 dB rule.

1 · The decibel ladder

Intensity I is the sound power passing through each square meter, in W/m2. The faintest sound an average ear can detect has intensity I0 = 10−12 W/m2, the threshold of hearing, which we call 0 dB. Every step of 10 dB up the ladder is ten times the intensity.

The count-the-tens ruleLevel in dB = 10 × (number of powers of ten above I0). You can do every problem in this note with this rule, without a log key.

A sound has intensity 10−6 W/m2. What is its level?

10−6 is six powers of ten above 10−12, so 60 dB.

Clapping read 30 dB above talking. How many times more intense was the clapping?

30 dB is three steps of 10 dB, so 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 times more intense.

Is a 0 dB sound silent?

No. 0 dB is the threshold intensity I0, the quietest sound an average ear can detect.

Can a sound be −10 dB?

Yes. It is one-tenth of the threshold intensity, too faint for most people to hear.

2 · Is twice as many twice as loud?

Predict: one person humming reads X dB. What will two people read? The whole class?

Two people: X + 3 dB. Twenty people: about X + 13 dB. Not double the decibels.

Put one phone in the middle of the group, everyone about 1 m away. Hum one comfortable note at steady effort and read the steady value. Then repeat with 2, 4 and 8 people, then the whole class.

What we foundDoubling the intensity (×2) adds about 3 dB. Ten times the intensity (×10) adds 10 dB. A hundred times (×100) adds 20 dB. Your ear hears +10 dB as roughly twice as loud, so it takes about ten people humming to sound twice as loud as one.
Why humming and not phones?Several phones playing the same pure tone interfere, just like the speakers in Module 1, so the reading swings as you move. Voices are not in step with each other, so their intensities simply add, which is what 10 log(n) assumes.

3 · The inverse-square walk

A small source spreads its sound power P over a growing sphere of area 4πr2:

I = P / (4πr2)

Predict: if you double your distance r, what fraction of the intensity is left, and how many decibels does the level drop?

Doubling r leaves ¼ of the intensity, two halvings, so the level drops by about 6 dB.

In the hallway, one phone plays a steady tone or white noise at a fixed volume, pointed at the measuring phone. Measure at 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 m.

Explain: your measured drops were probably smaller than 6 dB. Why?

Walls, floor and ceiling reflect sound back toward the meter, so the measured intensity does not fall off as fast as the inverse-square law predicts for open space. Outdoors, the drop comes much closer to 6 dB.

4 · Practice problems

Use the count-the-tens rule and the ×2 → +3 dB rule.

Conversation has intensity 10−6 W/m2. What level is that in dB?

60 dB (six powers of ten above I0).

An 80 dB sound has what intensity?

80 dB is eight powers of ten above I0: 10−12+8 = 10−4 W/m2.

One violin plays at 70 dB. What level do two violins make? Ten violins?

Two: ×2 intensity, 73 dB. Ten: ×10 intensity, 80 dB. Not 140 dB.

A speaker reads 100 dB at 2 m. What does it read at 4 m? At 20 m?

2 → 4 m is one doubling: 94 dB. 2 → 20 m is ten times farther, so 1/100 of the intensity: −20 dB, 80 dB.

How many times more intense is 90 dB than 60 dB? About how many times louder does it sound?

30 dB is three powers of ten: 1000 times more intense. Each +10 dB sounds about twice as loud: 2 × 2 × 2 = about 8 times louder.

5 · How loud is too loud?

Hearing damage depends on level and time. The recommended limit is 85 dB for 8 hours. Every additional 3 dB doubles the intensity, so it halves the safe time.

Measure your earbuds at max volume (phone mic against the earbud). How long would that level be safe for?

CautionPhone meters are not calibrated. Differences between two readings on the same phone are trustworthy; the absolute number is only a rough estimate. Typical earbuds at max volume reach roughly 95–105 dB: minutes, not hours.
