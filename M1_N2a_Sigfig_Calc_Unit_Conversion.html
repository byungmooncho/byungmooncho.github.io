Sig Figs in Calculations, Unit Conversion, and Unit Tracking

Synopsis Every measurement carries some uncertainty — how precisely your instrument can read a value. This note covers how to find that uncertainty, report it as %error (precision error from an instrument, or accuracy error against an expected value), and how significant figures survive (or don't) when you add, subtract, multiply, or divide measured numbers. It finishes with unit conversion and unit tracking — using conversion factors and checking that a formula's units make sense.
Performance Expectations By the end of this note, you will be able to…
  • PE1 Determine measurement uncertainty from an instrument's smallest marked interval, and compute the resulting precision %error.
  • PE2 Distinguish precision from accuracy, and compute accuracy %error by comparing a measured value to an expected (theoretical) value.
  • PE3 Apply sig-fig rules in calculations: match the smallest sig-fig count for multiplication/division, and match the least precise decimal place for addition/subtraction.
  • PE4 Convert a measured quantity between units using a conversion factor.
  • PE5 Track units through a formula to confirm a derived unit is correct (e.g., verify that N reduces to kg·m/s²).
every measurement has an uncertainty
💡You already know this

You already round results to match your least-precise input, even if you've never named the rule:

🌡️ A thermometer marked every 2° You'd estimate to the nearest degree, not claim tenths of a degree you can't actually read.
🍳 "A pinch" of salt in a recipe Even with an exact cup of flour, the whole recipe's precision is limited by the vaguest ingredient.
📶 A GPS "accurate to ±5 m" circle That's the app reporting its own uncertainty — exactly like a %error.

In this note, we answer four questions

  1. How do we determine the uncertainty of a measurement from an instrument's smallest markings?
  2. What's the difference between precision error and accuracy error?
  3. How do sig-fig rules change between addition/subtraction and multiplication/division?
  4. How do we convert units and verify that a formula's units are correct?

Key relationships for this note

  • 📏 %error = (uncertainty / measurement) × 100 precision error — from an instrument's smallest marking
  • 🎯 %error = |expected − measured| / expected × 100 accuracy error — comparing to a theoretical/expected value
  • 🔄 value × (desired unit / starting unit) unit conversion — multiply by a conversion factor equal to 1
  • ✅ substitute base units into the formula unit tracking — confirm a derived unit (e.g., N = kg·m/s²)
